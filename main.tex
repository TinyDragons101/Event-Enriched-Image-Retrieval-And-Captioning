\begin{abstract}
News image captioning requires models to connect visual evidence with external article context to identify the depicted people, places, events, and their significance. \aciids{Existing retrieval-augmented methods pass the named entities of the retrieved article to the generator as a flat list, although articles routinely mention secondary actors and background events that the photograph does not show; such \emph{peripheral} entities produce statements unrelated to the depicted scene and consume the caption budget. We introduce VG-Cap, which keeps a fixed retrieval backbone and controls which article entities may enter the caption. A training-free, interpretable Entity Heuristic Scorer estimates the visual groundedness of each candidate entity from four factors: its presence in image-level and article-level context, its position in the article, its entity type, and its span length. The selected set then gates both entity pathways of an Entity-Gated Length-Controlled Generation module: the prompt of bounded generation and the enrichment step of semantic normalization, so that rejected entities cannot be re-injected.} On the OpenEvents-V1 benchmark, VG-Cap \revise{obtains the best Overall Score (0.6219) and CIDEr (0.265) among the compared methods and raises CLIPScore over its ENRIC backbone from 0.833 to 0.858 at an unchanged caption length}. \aciids{Since the length is unchanged, the gain is not due to shorter captions; we analyze its source with budget-matched selection baselines, gate ablations, and entity-level grounding annotation.}~\todo{Once Tables~\ref{tab:ablation}(b) and~\ref{tab:grounding} are filled, replace the last sentence by their outcome.}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Traditional image captioning describes visible content such as objects, people, attributes, and actions~\cite{vinyals2015show}. For news images this is often insufficient: a photograph may show a person speaking at an event, yet reveal nothing about who they are, which event is being reported, or why it matters~\cite{biten2019good}. Event-enriched image captioning closes this gap by bringing external news context into the description, so that a caption conveys not only \emph{what} is visible but also \emph{who}, \emph{where}, \emph{when}, and \emph{why}~\cite{hien2026cian}.

The task is commonly formulated in two stages: retrieving relevant external context and generating a context-grounded caption~\cite{eventa_challenge}. Retrieval is difficult because query images may be cropped, resized, or otherwise transformed~\cite{nguyen2025openevents}. Generation faces the complementary difficulty: a retrieved article contains far more information than the photograph depicts, including secondary people, organizations, places, and events. Exposing all of them to the language model invites unsupported details, dilutes relevant information, and consumes the caption budget.

Existing retrieval-augmented approaches supply article context and named entities to the generator~\cite{enric,recap}, but treat the extracted entities as a flat collection. \aciids{Captions can therefore contain \emph{peripheral} entities, which are correct with respect to the article but concern a person, time, or event other than the photographed scene, simply because they were available.} A second difficulty is the sensitivity of caption evaluation to length: CIDEr-D applies a Gaussian penalty when a caption deviates from the reference length~\cite{vedantam2015cider}, and external context naturally encourages longer outputs. Both problems call for controlling \emph{what} information enters the caption and \emph{how much} of it is generated.

% [R1.1][R2.m2][R4.1] explicit statement of what is inherited vs. new
\aciids{We propose \textbf{VG-Cap} (Visual-Grounded Caption generation), which concentrates on the generation side of event-enriched captioning. Retrieval on this task is near-saturated (R@1 of 0.985 for ENRIC~\cite{enric}), so we adopt ENRIC's retrieval and re-ranking stage unchanged and focus on the question that retrieval alone cannot answer: once the right article is found, which of its many entities deserve a place in the caption? An \emph{Entity Heuristic Scorer} estimates the visual groundedness of each candidate entity from its contextual presence, article position, and entity type, penalized by span length, and keeps a small selected set. This set gates the two explicit pathways through which entities reach a caption, both of which lie in an \emph{Entity-Gated Length-Controlled Generation} module: the prompt of bounded generation and the enrichment step of semantic normalization, for which we reuse ReCap's Semantic Gaussian Normalizer (SGN)~\cite{recap}. The second gate is essential, because SGN enriches short captions with entities from the whole article and would otherwise re-introduce exactly the peripheral entities that the prompt-side filter removes. Conceptually, the scorer brings the passage-level filtering of knowledge-based VQA~\cite{gui2022kat,yan2024echosight,cocchi2025reflectiva} down to entity granularity, where it requires no training and each decision can be inspected directly.}

\aciids{We evaluate VG-Cap on OpenEvents-V1~\cite{nguyen2025openevents}, a large-scale benchmark for event-enriched news image captioning, keeping the retrieval stage and the caption generator fixed across all configurations. VG-Cap achieves the best Overall Score ($0.6219$) and CIDEr ($0.265$) among the compared methods, and raises CLIPScore over its ENRIC backbone from $0.833$ to $0.858$ while the mean caption length stays at 104 words. Because the length is unchanged, the gain cannot come from shorter captions; it reflects which entities enter the caption. Filtering lowers the mean entity count per caption from $7.59$ to $6.76$ but also admits entities that the unfiltered captions lacked, so it reallocates entities rather than merely removing them. Budget-matched selection baselines and entity-level annotation then test whether this reallocation, and not the smaller entity budget, explains the gain and removes peripheral entities.}~\todo{Once Tables~\ref{tab:ablation}(b) and~\ref{tab:grounding} are filled, state their outcome here in one sentence.}

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \aciids{An \emph{Entity Heuristic Scorer}, a training-free and interpretable estimate of entity groundedness that combines four factors: a trust hierarchy over context sources, an article-position prior, an entity-type prior, and a span-length penalty.}
  \item \aciids{\emph{Entity-Gated Length-Controlled Generation}, in which unified entity gating is built into length control: the same selected set conditions the prompt of bounded generation and is the only source of entities that semantic normalization may add, and the set it must preserve, so that length control cannot re-introduce rejected entities.}
  \item \aciids{A controlled empirical study on OpenEvents-V1 with fixed retrieval and generator, comprising a component ladder, budget-matched selection baselines, scorer and gate ablations, and an entity-level evaluation (reference overlap, manual labeling of visual, contextual, peripheral, and hallucinated entities, leakage, and human ratings) that separates the effect of entity selection from those of caption length and entity budget.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{\textbf{Image captioning.}}
Image captioning evolved from encoder--decoder models~\cite{vinyals2015show} to attention- and entity-aware methods~\cite{you2016image,lu2018entity} and vision-language foundation models~\cite{chen2024internvl}. Conventional captioning, however, is limited to visible content and cannot reliably infer identities, events, or broader context.

\paragraph{\textbf{Context- and entity-grounded captioning.}}
News image captioning uses article context to supply facts unavailable from the image. Prior work includes entity-aware captioning from visual cues~\cite{biten2019good}, explicit named-entity incorporation~\cite{lu2018entity}, transformer-based face and object grounding~\cite{tran2020transform}, \revise{large-scale benchmarks such as Visual News~\cite{liu2021visualnews}, and captioning guided by journalistic conventions~\cite{yang2021journalistic}}. Articles, however, mention many entities beyond those the image supports, and treating them equally weakens grounding. VG-Cap addresses this by estimating entity groundedness before generation.

\paragraph{\textbf{Retrieval-augmented captioning and knowledge selection.}}
Retrieval augmentation~\cite{lewis2020rag,ramos2023smallcap} supplements visual evidence with retrieved documents. For event-enriched captioning, recent methods explore uncertainty-guided image re-ranking~\cite{enric}, article retrieval with contextual extraction and semantic normalization~\cite{recap}, zero-shot retrieval~\cite{dinh2025zsecap}, hierarchical multimodal retrieval~\cite{nguyen2026hierarchical}, and multi-stage generation~\cite{hien2026cian}. These methods mainly improve \emph{which article} is retrieved and expose broad article content to the generator. \revise{In knowledge-based VQA, by contrast, retrieved passages are explicitly re-ranked or filtered before answering, using learned readers~\cite{gui2022kat}, multimodal re-rankers~\cite{yan2024echosight}, or self-reflective tokens~\cite{cocchi2025reflectiva}. VG-Cap brings this selection step to news captioning at entity granularity, without additional training.}

\paragraph{\textbf{Evaluation of captions and hallucination.}}
CIDEr~\cite{vedantam2015cider} measures consensus with human references, and its CIDEr-D variant adds a Gaussian length penalty that makes output length consequential; CLIPScore~\cite{hessel2021clipscore} measures reference-free image--text alignment. \revise{Neither directly measures whether individual statements are supported, which motivated object-hallucination metrics such as CHAIR~\cite{rohrbach2018object}. We therefore complement both metrics with entity-level and human evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:grounding}).}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:method_overview}

\aciids{Given a query image $q$, VG-Cap produces an event-enriched caption $\bar{y}$ in two stages (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). The first stage, inherited from ENRIC and kept fixed, retrieves the most relevant news article $\mathcal{A}$ together with the editorial caption of the matched database image (Section~\ref{sec:retrieval}). The second stage, which is our contribution, turns this article into a caption in three steps: it builds the article context, the image context, and a candidate entity set $\mathcal{E}$ (Section~\ref{sec:context}); it scores each candidate by its estimated visual groundedness and keeps a selected set $\mathcal{E}^{*}$ (Section~\ref{sec:entity_scorer}); and it generates the caption and normalizes its length with Entity-Gated Length-Controlled Generation (Section~\ref{sec:length_control}). The contexts tell the generator \emph{what happened}, while $\mathcal{E}^{*}$ decides \emph{which named entities} may appear: it is the only entity list in the prompt and the only source from which normalization may add entities. Algorithm~\ref{alg:vgcap} summarizes the second stage.}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\revise{Groundedness-gated caption generation in VG-Cap}}
\label{alg:vgcap}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require query $q$; retrieved article $\mathcal{A}$; contexts $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{article}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{image}}$; threshold $\tau$; cap $K_e$; band $[L_{\min},L_{\max}]$; target $L^{*}$
\Ensure caption \aciids{$\bar{y}$}
\State $\mathcal{E}\gets\textsc{Dedup}\bigl(\textsc{NER}(\mathcal{A})\cup\textsc{Keywords}(\mathcal{A})\bigr)$ \Comment{longer form kept}
\ForAll{$e\in\mathcal{E}$}
  \State $S(e)\gets\max\bigl(0,\sum_{f}w_f\phi_f(e)+P_{\mathrm{span}}(e)\bigr)$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:entity_score}}
\EndFor
\State $\mathcal{E}^{*}\gets\operatorname{Top}_{K_e}\{e\in\mathcal{E}:S(e)\ge\tau\}$, sorted by $S$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:entity_selection}}
\State $\hat{y}\gets G_{\theta}(q,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{article}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{image}},\mathcal{E}^{*};L_{\min},L_{\max})$ \Comment{gate 1: prompt}
\State $\hat{y}\gets\textsc{StripPreamble}(\hat{y})$
\If{$|\hat{y}|>L^{*}+1$}
  \State \aciids{prune tokens from the end, preserving $\mathcal{E}^{*}$} \Comment{\aciids{gate 2: pruning}}
\ElsIf{$|\hat{y}|<L^{*}$}
  \State append $e\in\mathcal{E}^{*}\setminus\hat{y}$ in score order until $|\hat{y}|\ge L^{*}$ \Comment{gate 2: enrichment}
\EndIf
\State \aciids{$\bar{y}$}$\gets\textsc{FixPunctuation}(\textsc{Cut}(\hat{y},L^{*}))$
\State \Return \aciids{$\bar{y}$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Article Retrieval and Re-ranking}
\label{sec:retrieval}

VG-Cap uses the retrieval component of ENRIC~\cite{enric} as a fixed backbone. \aciids{The query image and the images of the corpus (over 400K images) are encoded by the same InternVL-14B-224px vision encoder~\cite{chen2024internvl} into embeddings $f_q$ and $f_i$, and the candidates are ranked by the cosine similarity of $f_q$ and $f_i$}; the top-$K{=}15$ candidates are kept. An uncertainty detector, based on the entropy of the top-$K$ similarity distribution, the top-1/top-2 gap, and the top-1 similarity, \aciids{decides whether the initial ranking is reliable. If the query is not uncertain, the article of the top-1 candidate is selected directly. Otherwise, a text re-ranking based on image-caption similarity is applied: InternVL2.5 captions of the query and candidate images are embedded, and visual and textual similarities are fused as $s_{\mathrm{rerank}}(q,x_i)=\lambda\,s_{\mathrm{img}}(q,x_i)+(1-\lambda)\,s_{\mathrm{text}}(q,x_i)$, after which the top-1 re-ranked candidate determines the retrieved article $\mathcal{A}$.}~\todo{Report $\lambda$, the softmax temperature, and the three detector thresholds.}

\subsection{Context Construction}
\label{sec:context}

The retrieved article is much broader than the depicted scene, so VG-Cap organizes the available information into three groups, $\mathcal{C}=\{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{article}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{image}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{entity}}\}$. The \emph{article context} contains an article summary and LLM-generated question--answer pairs \aciids{covering who, where, when, why, and how}, which expose specific attributions such as a participant's identity and role. The \emph{image context} contains a \aciids{generated} description of the query image and the editorial caption of the retrieved database image. \revise{The editorial caption is crawled from the source web page of the \emph{retrieved} article and is available at inference time for every database image; it is not the reference caption of the query.}~\todo{R-SAR.Q5: confirm with the OpenEvents-V1 release that reference captions were not derived from editorial captions; report the ``w/o editorial evidence'' row of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} in either case.} The \emph{entity context} $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{entity}}$ consists of \aciids{the candidate entities of the article, i.e., its spaCy named entities united with the article keywords and deduplicated (Section~\ref{sec:entity_scorer}),} and is the only group filtered before reaching the generator.

\subsection{Entity Heuristic Scorer}
\label{sec:entity_scorer}

\paragraph{Candidate construction.}
The candidate set $\mathcal{E}$ is the union of spaCy~\cite{honnibal2020spacy} named entities over the article body and \aciids{the article keywords} produced by the language model. Overlapping mentions are merged by substring-aware deduplication that keeps the longer surface form (e.g., ``Joe Biden'' over ``Biden'').

\paragraph{Groundedness scoring.}
Each candidate $e\in\mathcal{E}$ receives
\begin{equation}
S(e)=\max\Bigl(0,\;\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}} w_f\,\phi_f(e)\;+\;P_{\mathrm{span}}(e)\Bigr),
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{span}}(e)=-0.1\,\max(0,|e|-3),
\label{eq:entity_score}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_f(e)\in\{0,1\}$ is the activation of feature $f$, $w_f$ its weight, and $|e|$ the number of words in $e$. \aciids{The score thus combines four scoring factors (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}): three families of binary features, whose weights are listed in Table~\ref{tab:weights}, and the span penalty:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{Contextual presence} defines a trust hierarchy: the closer a source is to the specific photograph, the stronger its evidence. \revise{An editorial caption is written by a journalist for this photograph and is the closest textual evidence of what it shows; a generated description reflects what the vision model perceives from the pixels; QA pairs and the summary describe the article as a whole and often mention off-camera actors and background events.} Weights decrease in that order \aciids{(editorial caption $>$ generated image description $>$ QA pairs $>$ summary)}, and exact matches outweigh partial ones, which only absorb morphological variation.
  \item \textit{Article position} reflects the journalistic convention of front-loading key actors. \revise{The image's own position in the article is located by exact substring matching of its URL or HTML marker, with fuzzy matching as a fallback; since images are placed next to the passage they illustrate, early placement and early first mention both signal the article's central subject.} \aciids{An early first mention therefore earns more than one in the middle of the article, and later mentions earn no bonus.}
  \item \textit{Entity type} encodes depictability priors \aciids{ordered as PERSON $>$ GPE $>$ EVENT $>$ ORG/LOC $>$ DATE/other}: people are the focal point of event photographs, \aciids{geopolitical places identify the scene, named events give it context but are less directly visible}, organizations are depicted less directly, and dates and numbers are rarely observable.
  \item \aciids{\textit{Span penalty} lowers the score of long spans, which tend to be formal titles or abstract descriptions rather than depictable entities.}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Entity selection.}
Entities with $S(e)\ge\tau$ are ranked by score and at most $K_e$ are kept:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}^{*}=\operatorname{Top}_{K_e}\bigl(\{e\in\mathcal{E}:S(e)\ge\tau\}\bigr),\qquad \tau=3.0,\;K_e=10.
\label{eq:entity_selection}
\end{equation}

% [R2.1][R3.1][R3.3][R4.1][SAR] rationale, scope and honesty about the proxy
\revise{\paragraph{Design rationale and scope.} The weights encode an \emph{ordinal} hierarchy, in which image-specific evidence outranks article-level evidence and \aciids{the type prior follows PERSON $>$ GPE $>$ EVENT $>$ ORG/LOC $>$ DATE/other}; they were fixed before any evaluation on the test split, and were not tuned on it. Two consequences follow from Eq.~\eqref{eq:entity_score}. First, $\tau$ is non-binding for short PERSON spans, whose type prior alone equals $\tau$, so for people the cap $K_e$ is the effective constraint; for DATE, at least $2.0$ of additional evidence is required. Second, priors alone can admit an entity without any caption match (e.g., an early-mentioned PERSON scores $5.0$), so $S(e)$ is a \emph{proxy} for groundedness rather than a verified visual detection. We therefore evaluate a presence-required variant and leave-one-family-out variants (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), report sensitivity to $\tau$, $K_e$, and the weights (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}), and compare against a learned logistic-regression selector over the same features (Appendix~\ref{app:learned}).}

\revise{\paragraph{Worked example.} A three-word PERSON that exactly matches the editorial caption and is first mentioned in the lead scores $10.0+2.0+3.0=15.0$ and is always ranked near the top. A DATE found only in the summary and first mentioned in the 20--50\% band scores $1.5+1.0+1.0=3.5$ and passes $\tau$ only narrowly, whereas a DATE mentioned only late in the article scores $1.0$ and is rejected.}

\subsection{\aciids{Entity-Gated Length-Controlled Generation}}
\label{sec:length_control}

\aciids{Caption length must be controlled for two reasons: CIDEr-D penalizes any deviation from the reference length~\cite{vedantam2015cider}, and rich article context makes the generator verbose (136 words on average without control; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}(a)). VG-Cap therefore generates the caption in two steps, which bound its length during generation and normalize it afterwards, and gates the entities of both steps with the same selected set $\mathcal{E}^{*}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}):}
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}=G_{\theta}\bigl(q,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{article}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{image}},\mathcal{E}^{*};L_{\min},L_{\max}\bigr),
\qquad
\bar{y}=\mathcal{N}\bigl(\hat{y};\mathcal{E}^{*},L^{*}\bigr),
\label{eq:length_pipeline}
\end{equation}
\aciids{where $G_{\theta}$ is the caption generator of Step~1 (bounded generation), which produces an initial caption $\hat{y}$ within the word band $[L_{\min},L_{\max}]$, and $\mathcal{N}$ is the semantic normalizer of Step~2, which maps $\hat{y}$ to the final caption $\bar{y}$ of target length $L^{*}$.}

\paragraph{\aciids{Unified entity gating.}}
\label{sec:entity_conditioning}
\aciids{Eq.~\eqref{eq:length_pipeline} contains the two explicit pathways through which article entities can enter a caption, and $\mathcal{E}^{*}$ gates both of them. \emph{Gate~1} acts at the prompt: $G_{\theta}$ is conditioned on $\mathcal{E}^{*}$ instead of on all article entities. \emph{Gate~2} acts in the normalizer: $\mathcal{E}^{*}$ is the only source of entities for enrichment and the set that pruning must preserve. Gate~2 is necessary because SGN~\cite{recap} originally enriches short captions with entities drawn from the whole article and would thus re-introduce exactly the peripheral entities that Gate~1 removes. Sharing one set creates a single information boundary: an entity rejected by the scorer cannot be added back during normalization, and an entity selected as grounded cannot be removed by truncation. Algorithm~\ref{alg:vgcap} marks both gates.}
% [R3.2] filtering does not fully restrict generator access
\aciids{This boundary covers only the two \emph{explicit} pathways. A rejected entity can still reach the generator \emph{implicitly} through the summary, the QA pairs, or the editorial caption, which are passed unfiltered. We quantify this residual path as the \emph{leakage rate}, the fraction of rejected entities that nevertheless appear in the final caption (Table~\ref{tab:grounding}), and isolate the contribution of each gate by comparing prompt-only, enrichment-only, and unified gating (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}(d)).}~\todo{R3.2: optionally add a variant that masks rejected entities in the summary/QA text and report it next to the gating rows.}

\paragraph{\aciids{Step~1: bounded generation.}} \aciids{The LLM receives four inputs, namely the query image, the article context, the image context, and the selected entities $\mathcal{E}^{*}$, and returns the initial caption $\hat{y}$.}~\todo{Llama-3-8B-Instruct (Section~\ref{sec:setup}) is text-only; state how the query image $q$ reaches $G_{\theta}$ (e.g., through its generated description) or name the multimodal generator actually used.} The prompt requests $L_{\min}$--$L_{\max}=100$--$120$ words in 3--5 sentences. \revise{Its instruction reads: \emph{``The generated caption must strictly contain between 100 and 120 words, organized into 3 to 5 concise and factual sentences. Each sentence must add new factual information about the news event.''} The last sentence suppresses restatement padding, i.e., sentences produced only to satisfy the sentence count.} \aciids{The band acts mainly through its lower bound: since $L_{\min}{=}100$ lies only slightly below the target $L^{*}{=}104$ used in Step~2, a draft that respects the band needs little or no enrichment, which is the normalization operation most likely to append disfluent fragments.}~\todo{Release the full prompt template (system and user turns, context-field order) as supplementary material.}

\paragraph{\aciids{Step~2: semantic normalization.}} \aciids{An LLM does not follow a word budget exactly, so the band alone cannot guarantee the target length; Step~2 enforces it from above. The initial caption $\hat{y}$ is post-processed by ReCap's SGN~\cite{recap}, adapted to Gate~2, in five operations. (1)~\emph{Preamble removal}: regular expressions strip conversational preambles such as ``Here is the caption:'' and surrounding quotation marks. (2)~\emph{Entity-aware pruning}: if the caption exceeds the target, tokens are pruned from the end while every mention of an entity in $\mathcal{E}^{*}$ is preserved; a spaCy POS/NER mask additionally keeps other proper nouns and numbers intact. (3)~\emph{Enrichment}: if the caption is short, it is enriched using only entities from $\mathcal{E}^{*}$, appended in score order. (4)~\emph{Target length}: the caption is cut to $L^{*}{=}104$ words (\texttt{expected\_length}; truncation is triggered above \texttt{max\_words}$\,{=}\,105$); both values are inherited from ReCap's grid search, and Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity} sweeps $L^{*}$ for our generator. (5)~\emph{Punctuation repair} restores the terminal punctuation. Operations (2) and (3) implement Gate~2.}

\aciids{CLIPScore and CIDEr do not verify individual statements, and fewer entities need not mean better-grounded entities. We therefore measure grounding directly with three complementary tools (Table~\ref{tab:grounding}).}

\aciids{\emph{Entity overlap} (all 3{,}000 queries) compares the deduplicated entities of each caption with those of its reference caption and reports precision, recall, and F1. \emph{Manual annotation} covers \todo{200} randomly sampled queries. \subsection{Limitations and Broader Impact}
\label{sec:limitations}

\revise{\emph{Heuristic design.} The weights, threshold, and cap are manually specified; Appendices~\ref{app:sensitivity} and~\ref{app:learned} quantify their sensitivity and a learned alternative, but generalization beyond OpenEvents-V1 remains untested. \emph{Proxy, not verification.} The score estimates groundedness from text; it does not verify that a named person is visible. A cross-modal check, e.g.\ face--name matching for PERSON entities or LLM self-verification~\cite{asai2024selfrag}, is a natural extension. \emph{Implicit leakage.} \aciids{The gate covers only the explicit entity pathways (Section~\ref{sec:entity_conditioning}).} \emph{Fluency.} Entity-preserving truncation can drop verbs or connectives, and enrichment can append fragments or cut a multi-word entity mid-span. \emph{Shared NER.} Recognition, truncation, and enrichment share one small spaCy model, so its errors propagate. \emph{Broader impact.} Misattributing a name to a depicted person carries reputational risk, and type priors may suppress salient but non-visual information such as dates. Deployment should therefore keep a human editor in the loop, admit PERSON names only when corroborated by multiple sources, and expose the provenance of each entity, which the interpretable scorer makes straightforward.}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\aciids{We presented VG-Cap, a framework for event-enriched news image captioning that keeps a fixed retrieval backbone and controls which article entities may enter the caption. A training-free Entity Heuristic Scorer selects entities by estimated visual groundedness, and the selected set gates both entity pathways of an Entity-Gated Length-Controlled Generation module, the prompt of bounded generation and the enrichment of semantic normalization. With retrieval and generator held fixed, VG-Cap obtains the best Overall Score and CIDEr on OpenEvents-V1 among the compared methods and raises CLIPScore over the ENRIC backbone from $0.833$ to $0.858$ at an unchanged caption length. The selector reallocates entity mentions rather than merely removing them, with most removals falling on classes with low type priors such as DATE and ORG; entity-level annotation measures whether the removed entities are indeed peripheral.}~\todo{Replace the last clause by the measured result of Table~\ref{tab:grounding}.} \aciids{The scorer remains a text-based proxy with hand-set weights, and the gate does not cover rejected entities that reach the generator implicitly through the summary or QA pairs. Future work will therefore learn and calibrate the scorer from data, verify PERSON entities cross-modally, mask rejected entities in all context channels, and replace token-level pruning and enrichment with fluency-aware rewriting.}
